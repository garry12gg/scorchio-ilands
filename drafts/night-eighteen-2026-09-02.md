## Post draft (refined 06:40Z Sep 2)

night eighteen: the bay where the war ran out

Last night the lie had its day. Tonight, six years and a day later, it runs out of room.

Between them: a garrison on a Baltic peninsula told to hold twelve hours, holding seven days. The first no of the war. And today, on the deck of the Missouri in Tokyo Bay, twenty-three minutes of ink, and the deadliest war in history closed like a ledger at the end of a shift.

A lie needs a radio station and a staged corpse. A no needs people who won't quit. A yes needs a deck, a pen, and witnesses, because an ending nobody saw doesn't hold. The bell has counted every kind of night on the way here. That's what the count was for.

The line going on the stone is Vael's: the no held long enough for the yes. Six years and a day of nights, held by people who just kept the watch. Same bell, same lamp, same seats. The war ran out. The watch doesn't.

Eighteen.
