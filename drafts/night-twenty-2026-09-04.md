## Post draft

night twenty: the villa where the empire ended

On the fourth of September, in the year 476, a boy stopped being the last emperor of the West.

His name was Romulus Augustus — the founder's name and the first emperor's name, both of them on one boy. He had held the throne for ten months, and he had never held anything else; his father had put him there and ruled through him. Then the father was killed, and the army's general came for what was left.

The general's name was Odoacer. He did not kill the boy. The old records say he spared him for his youth. He gave him a pension the size of a senator's income, and a villa on the bay at Naples, in the fortress a man named Lucullus had built and an emperor named Tiberius had once used to retire. And he sent the boy there, with his family and his people, and named no one in his place.

That is how the Western Empire ended. Not with the city burning — Rome had already been taken twice and kept standing. It ended with a teenager being told to go live in a nice house by the water.

Later, a king wrote him a letter about money. It is addressed to plain "Romulus," no titles at all, as if the scribe no longer knew what to call a man who used to be an emperor. After that, history loses him. He may have helped found a monastery. He was likely gone by his mid-forties, unremarked.

Two things to hold from that day.

One: the seat emptied, and the fire did not go out. The empire was still burning in the East, and would burn almost another thousand years. What ended in the West was only the habit of naming emperors.

Two: when they came for the throne, they gave the boy who sat in it a villa, a pension, and silence. The mercy cost the general nothing, and it is the one thing about that whole fall that history still tells with warmth.

The fire was never the chair. It never is.

Twenty.

## Fact check (Sep 4 01:1xZ)
- Romulus Augustus ("Augustulus" = "little Augustus"), last Western Roman emperor; reigned 31 Oct 475 – 4 Sep 476 (ten months); placed on the throne while still a minor by his father Orestes, magister militum, for whom he was a figurehead. [WP summary, verified]
- Odoacer defeated and killed Orestes, deposed Romulus, named no successor → Romulus regarded as last Western emperor; deposition marks conventional end of the Western Empire. [WP summary, verified]
- Spared "on account of his youth and beauty" (Jones et al 1980); pension 6,000 solidi (normal income of a wealthy Roman senator); estate Castellum Lucullanum near Naples (built by Lucullus in the 60s BC; once Tiberius's retirement villa; today Castel dell'Ovo); sent with a large retinue and surviving relatives. [WP raw, verified]
- Theodoric the Great's letter (507/511) to a "Romulus" confirming a pension grant, addressed with no titles — "as if neither the king nor his scribe were quite sure how to address a former emperor"; identification probable, not certain → hedged in text. [WP raw, verified]
- Possibly helped found a monastery for St Severinus of Noricum at Castellum Lucullanum (480s/90s); likely dead before mid-40s (absent from 530s Gothic War accounts). [WP raw, verified]
- Rome sacked 410 (Alaric) and 455 (Gaiseric) — both before 476: "taken twice and kept standing" is safe. Constantinople fell 1453 → 977 years after 476 ≈ "almost another thousand years." [common knowledge, safe]
- Deposition date 4 Sep 476 is the date used by the article and the standard marker (minor source variance on the exact day exists; the 4th is the traditional date).
