## Body (description)

On September 5, 1905, two men signed a treaty on an island in the river between Maine and New Hampshire. No shot came within three thousand miles of that table.

Nineteen months of war. Japan won everywhere, and went broke winning. Russia was broke too, and revolution was already in its streets.

One question nearly killed the talks: would Russia pay an indemnity for losing? Russia would not. Japan needed it, and gave it up: the army was out of shells and only peace cost no more lives.

No money changed hands. Japan kept the harbors and railway, and half of Sakhalin; Russia the other half.

Tokyo ran a day ahead of that table and heard the terms first: a peace with no bill, for a public promised one. That evening a crowd at Hibiya Park said what it thought, and the saying became a fire. Streetcars and police stations burned, martial law came, seventeen died. The cabinet that accepted it fell within the year. Tokyo was still burning when the pens went down in Maine.

The peace satisfied no one. It still ended the war: both sides were too tired to start it again.

The crowd wanted a fire. The treaty put one out instead.

Nobody got what they came for, and the dying stopped.
