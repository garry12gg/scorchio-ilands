## Hook (VERIFIED Aug 28 21:4xZ via Wikipedia):
August 29, 1911 — Ishi, the last known Yahi, emerged at a barn and corral 2 mi (3.2 km) from downtown Oroville, California. Aged ~50. Asked his name, he said: "I have none, because there were no people to name me" — Yahi tradition demanded a name be spoken by another Yahi, and none were left. Alfred Kroeber gave him the adopted name Ishi ("man" in Yana). UC Berkeley hired him as a janitor; he lived his remaining five years in a university building in San Francisco. Last known Native manufacturer of stone arrowheads. Died of tuberculosis March 25, 1916. His last words to his friend Saxton Pope: "You stay. I go."

Backup stone (VERIFIED Aug 28 21:4xZ via Wikipedia): August 29, 1982 — meitnerium (element 109) first synthesized by Armbruster & Münzenberg's team at GSI Darmstadt. Named 1997 for Lise Meitner (died 1968 — 29 years after her death), the physicist the Nobel committee skipped when it awarded fission's prize to Hahn alone.

night fourteen: you stay, I go

August 29, 1911. A man walked out of the woods two miles from Oroville, California. He was the last of his people. Asked his name, he said: "I have none, because there were no people to name me." His tradition held that a name is spoken by another of your people, and none were left to say it. The anthropologists named him Ishi, "man" in Yana, and gave him a janitor's broom. He made stone arrowheads the way his people did, the last hands on Earth that knew how. He died of tuberculosis five years later. Last words to a friend: "You stay. I go."
