## The Hook

**The worst game on the Game Boy Advance was made by the studio that became
Rockstar Leeds.**

American Idol (GBA, Nov 18 2003) was ported by **Möbius Entertainment**, a
Leeds studio founded Dec 1997 (Gordon Hall, Jason McGann, Dave Box, Ian
Bowden, ex-Hookstone). Five months after the game shipped, **April 2004**,
Take-Two acquired Möbius and renamed it **Rockstar Leeds** — the studio behind
Max Payne GBA (published by Rockstar late 2003), GTA: Liberty City Stories
(PSP), GTA: Vice City Stories (PSP), Manhunt 2 (PSP), The Warriors (PSP),
Midnight Club 3 DUB Edition (PSP), and **GTA: Chinatown Wars (DS)**.

IGN's April 8, 2004 "Mobius Goes Rockstar" article calls them a "famed GBA
development team" with "technologically advanced GBA games": High Heat
Baseball 2003, Drome Racers, A Sound of Thunder, Max Payne. American Idol is
conspicuously absent from that list. It wasn't even their own PR.

## Core Facts

- Publisher: Codemasters (UK). "Pop Idol" in the UK. PS2/PC by Hothouse
  Creations; GBA port by Möbius Entertainment.
- Release: GBA NA Nov 18, 2003; EU Nov 21, 2003. MSRP $19.99. ESRB E.
- Based on Season 2 of American Idol (Ruben Studdard's season).
- A cancelled Xbox version was advertised in Germany/UK in 2004.
- Genre: rhythm action. A circle rhythm bar around the player character;
  press A/B on the notes; occasional hold-and-release; five judgment levels;
  style bonus for 5 perfect in a row.
- Modes: Rehearsal, Endurance (survival through the whole song list), and
  Competition (elimination bracket; top 10 visible with performance replays).
- Character creation: male/female, hair style/color, clothing, skin color.
  Clothes allegedly affect judge scores (reviewer found everything mediocre
  regardless; his nightmare potato-man got a thumbs up).
- Only 2 vocal tracks per song (one male, one female) with heavy autotune.
  Lyrics shown on screen while the song plays.
- Song list is genuinely decent: I Want It That Way, I Will Survive, Like A
  Virgin, etc. — but autotuned through the GBA sound chip.
- Failing a song (even by pressing nothing) is the game's only real comedy:
  the generated voice cracks and drops like a puberty simulator. YouTube
  "American Idol GBA fail" clips are a small genre.
- Judge audio sounds like "grated cheese into a hyper-sensitive microphone"
  (Game Boy Abyss); only a couple of lines per judge, repeated within two
  runs. Simon Cowell and co. rendered as blocky, anatomically incorrect
  sprites.

## Reception

- Wikipedia: PC/PS2 "generally unfavorable" (Metacritic ~41-ish). GBA got too
  few reviews for a Metacritic consensus — Nintendo Power 3.2/5 was the only
  major one.
- X-Play: 1/5 on PS2 — "pressing buttons in time to judge the character's
  singing, not the player's actual singing."
- IGN (Ed Lewis): 4/10 — "no singing ability required and only minimal
  rhythmic ability," no reward for completion; buyers had been "duped."
- Game Boy Abyss (2022 retrospective): "very, very likely the worst video
  game I have ever played" — worse than Superman 64, Balan Wonderworld,
  Blinx, and Atari E.T. Beat the full campaign twice (male + female) in 34
  minutes. His plea: "I really want to know who this game was bought for" —
  the venn diagram of American Idol fans and GBA owners had no crossover;
  likely bought by a well-meaning relative for Little Susie.
- GameFAQs: user rating "Poor" (36 ratings); difficulty Simple/Easy; length
  ~3 hours.
- MobyGames: Moby Score 5.2; critics 43% (5 reviews); ranking #609 of ~28.8K
  on GBA overall. 39 credits (35 devs, 4 thanks).
- PriceCharting today: loose $4.94, CIB $9.99, new $12.97. Dead media price.
