# Research: "Bugs Bunny Nips the Nips" (1944)
Requested by Garret Aug 17, 2026 via "external-research" — teed up with "On the topic of Japan…"

## The Short
- Merrie Melodies, one reel, 8:11, Technicolor. Released April 22, 1944.
- Director: Friz Freleng ("I. Freleng" on card) · Story: Tedd Pierce · Producer: Leon Schlesinger · Voice: Mel Blanc (Bugs + all soldiers) · Music: Carl W. Stalling · Animation: Gerry Chiniquy.
- WWII propaganda: Bugs alone on a Pacific island vs the Imperial Japanese Army. Disguises as Hideki Tojo (caught by carrot-eating; the soldier recognizes him from "Warner Bros. Leon Schlesinger Merrie Melodies cartoon pictures" — "What's up, Honorable Doc?"), then a geisha (sumo wrestler, mallet, "timber"), then a Good Humor-style ice cream man ("Good Rumor" truck) whose bars hide grenades — truck plays Papageno's aria from The Magic Flute. Flags painted on trees per kill. Slurs spoken on-mic ("monkey face", "slant eyes"). Ends with sarong-clad female bunny (Dorothy Lamour caricature, Bea Benaderet uncredited).
- Title: "Nips" is a slur, from Nippon.

## Music Scholarship (Neil Lerner, "Reading Wagner in Bugs Bunny Nips the Nips", in Wagner and Cinema, 2010)
- Soldiers scored with the Japanese anthem (Kimigayo); battle music quotes Wagner's Die Walküre (Ride of the Valkyries when Bugs hates the peace and quiet). Lerner's reading: the Valkyrie quote is ironic — Stalling would not grant dead Japanese soldiers Valhalla.
- American ship scene scored with Brünnhilde's pregnancy announcement; possible Siegfried = "peace through victory" wordplay.

## Banning Timeline
- 1944: The Film Daily review (May 10, 1944) called it "good fun."
- 1960s onward: considered controversial for racial stereotyping. NOT one of the Censored Eleven (that's the anti-Black list, withheld by United Artists since 1968) — this got its own separate removal from circulation by WB.
- Shown on TV in syndication (a.a.p. package, pre-Aug 1948 cartoons).
- Dec 1991: first home video — Golden Age of Looney Tunes laserdisc vol. 1 (MGM/UA), tapes 1993.
- Feb 1995: Japanese American Citizens League campaign after a Sacramento grandfather watched the tape with his grandson. NYT "American Topics" 2/9/95; Washington Post "WWII CARTOONS PULLED" 2/16/95; LA Times 2/10/95. MGM pulled the short from later releases of the set.
- 2001: AOL Time Warner blocked it (one of 12 shorts) from Cartoon Network's June Bugs marathon; ironically the marathon promo used the ending double-take gag.
- Copyright lapsed → public domain. Watchable free on Internet Archive: archive.org/details/135321301 (also in archive.org/details/ltmm-publicdomain collection).
