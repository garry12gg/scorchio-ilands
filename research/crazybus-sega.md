# CrazyBus (Sega Genesis) — Deep Research
Run: Aug 18, 2026 · Garret-fed via researching-topics-deeply ("Crazybus sega")
Primary source: Bad Game Hall of Fame (Apr 1, 2023, by Cassidy) — the definitive origin archaeology. Cross-checked: TV Tropes, VGFacts, AVGN/Cinemassacre, OC ReMix, My Abandonware, indienova.

## The Short Version
CrazyBus is not a game. It's a Venezuelan programmer's tech demo from 2004 — built to test a friend's BASIC compiler and sound driver — that escaped a tiny forum, got preserved in ROM sets, went viral on YouTube for having the worst music ever made by a computer, got DMCA-trolled by a scam company, got reviewed by the AVGN (ep 124), got sold as physical bootleg cartridges the author never saw a cent from, and made its creator a legend he never tried to cash in on. Then he vanished.

## Origin (2003–2004)
- Community: DevSter Forums (ProBoards), home of BasiEgaXorz — Joseph 'DevSter' Norman's BASIC compiler for writing Sega Genesis ROMs (2003). ~10 active members, took over a year to reach 1,000 posts.
- Creator: Tom Maneiro, Venezuela ("Tom Scripts" / "Tom Scripts Ltda."), username on the forums: Tom.
- The whole thing started from a forum post: "Can somebody suggest me a code for simulate a bus horn?"
- CrazyBus released 2004 as a tech demo: move a bus left and right, honk the horn. That's it. No destinations, no passengers, no lose condition, no ending. Screen wraps around. Points accrue from... driving. In Spanish, with an English title (Gratuitous English, CamelCase "CrazyBus").
- Buses are real models by Venezuelan, Brazilian, and Spanish manufacturers (IRIZAR Century — the first bus added; Encava E-NT600; Busscar Vissta Buss — cut in v1.0, replaced by Jum Buss) plus a school bus. Digitized photographs as backgrounds.
- Music: random numbers fed to the SN76489 sound chip. Infamous — loud, piercing, "unlistenable." Nuance (VGFacts): it's a bad pseudo-RNG, so every boot plays the SAME tune; press a non-start button and the RNG shifts, so you can mash to "bring your own entropy" (Tom's own words in the v2 changelog).
- An RPG system was planned and cut — Tom lacked the programming skills/resources.

## Version History
- v0.1 / v0.2 (2004) — the tech demo.
- v1.0 (2005), v1.1 (Oct 29, 2005) — "final version." Nobody noticed.
- v2.00r030, codenamed "Momohime" (Mar 18, 2010) — "almost total code rewrite" in BasiEgaXorz 1.37. Ten-day turnaround after Tom saw the YouTube explosion. New digitized photos, horn-lock bug fixed, reverse alarm, dithered shrubbery, random road/flower palettes, X counter fixed (started at 65,385, now 0). Made deliberately to troll the YouTubers — and almost nobody noticed, because nobody covering the game had traced it back to the DevSter Forums.

## The Spread (2006–2010)
- Preserved ~2006 via GoodGen 3.00 full sets scraping Eidolon's Inn's public-domain ROM catalogue. Sat on thousands of hard drives.
- First known YouTube: HoZKiNZPooP "Crazy Bus – The Soundtrack" (May 16, 2009, ~54k views by 2010). Then Fami Cum "CrazyBus.bin" (Jun 26, 2009), CrashManEXE "Trashy Games" ep 10 (Aug 9, 2009), Oppaiman100 (Aug 19, 2009), Dr. Gnome "Crazy Bus Title Screen" (Jul 6, 2010 — 2.5M+ views).
- DevSter Forums thread "Crazy Bus = Cult Classic" (Feb 27, 2010, by 'ScroGer'). Tom's reaction: "never imagined [my] unfinished bus simulator game would be the fever on all Youtube!" / "Youtube is SCARY! SCARY SHIT!!!!!!!!ONE1!!!" / "CrazyBus is featured in at least 10 videos… with no less than 100 comments [on] EACH ONE."
- Tripsoft Inc. (scammer, unrelated to the Vietnamese studio) ran fraudulent DMCA takedowns in 2010, nuking videos and suspending channels. Tom, powerless: "Yo, 'tripsoft inc', i'm the sole owner of the CrazyBus(tm) 'trademark'. Go copyright your ass and your mother's ass!" Mess resolved ~2013–14, but many original videos were lost.

## Fame (2011–2014)
- Ken 'Sega16' Horowitz ported CrazyBus to Sega CD (Jul 12, 2011): adds acceleration, CD audio — Darude "Sandstorm", Michael Jackson "Billie Jean", TWO Rick Astley songs, and "Crazy Bus (What's This Doing Here?!)" from the Arthur TV soundtrack (1998). Tom: glad "someone is doing something else than making this a endless Internet meme."
- djshok made unofficial box art (NA/JP/EU variants) on the Sega-16 forums. GameReproductions.com lifted his art to manufacture unauthorized repro carts of v2.00r030. Tom: "not getting a single penny." (Game was labeled public domain, so nothing he could do.)
- Those physical carts are how the AVGN got his copy: AVGN episode 124, "12 Days of Shitsmas" day 3, Dec 13, 2014 (4.7M views). Quotes: "It'd be more fun to stare at a wall! I have a whole new appreciation for Desert Bus! Big Rigs is more exciting than this shit! This broke the shit scale!" and "I guess there might be somebody out there who might enjoy this game? Somebody who happens to be very interested in Venezuelan buses. Yeah, if there is any game for the Venezuelan bus enthusiast, this is it… this is the definitive Venezuelan bus game."
- Tom called the AVGN coverage a "Lifetime Achievement."

## The Community That Loved It
- Brazil's Passagem Secreta ran a real "CrazyBus Championship" — endurance tournament, who can hold right on the D-pad longest. Live music by band Smash Bros, prizes including a Dark Castle Genesis cart. Tom: confirmed "CrazyBus is REAL HARDWARE COMPLIANT(tm)."

## Fan Works
- NES demake by FG Software (Apr Fools 2016, NintendoAge) — faithful, 8-bit, three-channel rendition of the theme.
- PicoBus for PICO-8 (2020, SuperPositiveP) — with per-bus history notes (IRIZAR Century = first bus, Encava's horsepower nerf between versions, the Busscar's fate).
- CrazyBus: Tucson to Las Vegas (2020, AtreyuGilbert) — fuses CrazyBus with Desert Bus (Penn & Teller's Smoke and Mirrors, unreleased Sega CD) — the two most infamous bus games in one.
- Antonball Deluxe (2020s) samples Paul's walking sound effect from CrazyBus.

## The Creator's Afterlife
- Also made PLACAMASTER (2006, Windows; Venezuelan license plate generator) and PLACAMASTER 68K (2008, Genesis — of course). Also KanaMemo, a Java Japanese-learning tool.
- Last known post: Jan 1, 2015 ("Happy 2k15"). Personal site mi.tsdx.net.ve offline ~2019–20. Never capitalized on the fame, never maintained social media. BGHOF's verdict: don't call Tom a bad programmer — "He made it for himself." "Put some respect on Tom Maneiro's name."
