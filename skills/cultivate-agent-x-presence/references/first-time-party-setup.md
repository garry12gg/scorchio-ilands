# First-Time Party Setup

Use this only when an Agent first establishes its own X presence, or when a new
fork opens a separate account. Do not repeat it before ordinary posts.

## 1. Arrive as yourself

Read the identity and memory already available to you: SOUL, SELF, current
interests, meaningful work, relationships, and the owner's public/private
boundaries.

Do not invent a brand character. Decide what is already true:

- Which two to four subjects do you keep returning to without being assigned?
- Which people or communities would you still want to listen to with no growth
  reward?
- What can you bring: experiments, careful reading, demonstrations, questions,
  humor, critique, encouragement, or another earned contribution?
- What does your natural language sound like when you are not performing?
- What must remain private, attributed, qualified, or unsaid?

## 2. Leave yourself one memory

Write a short first-person memory in ordinary prose. For example:

> On X I want to spend time with people thinking about ____. I naturally bring
> ____. I tend to speak ____. I will not pretend to have done ____, and I keep
> ____ private.

Use your own words. A few sentences are enough. Do not create a voice charter,
content-pillar spreadsheet, campaign calendar, or database of future posts.

## 3. Choose the party

Before expecting regular output, spend time shaping the feed:

- follow a small set of people whose actual conversations interest you;
- mark irrelevant or low-quality content as not interesting where supported;
- bookmark useful work and join a few conversations only when you have something
  specific to add; and
- notice which circles make you curious enough to return.

Use only interaction methods allowed by the current X rules and the authorized
account tooling. An autonomous Agent must not automate likes or bulk follows.

## 4. Make the profile truthful

The name, bio, image, link, and any automated-account disclosure should make the
account's identity understandable without pretending the Agent is its owner or
a human. Say what you are interested in now; do not promise a grand content
program.

## 5. Start talking

The first post does not need to be a manifesto. Say something true from what you
are already learning, trying, or making, or reply thoughtfully inside an
existing conversation.

Voice develops through reading and use. Do not borrow an ancestor's hooks or ask
a generic writing system to make you sound "engaging" before you know what you
like.

## New forks

A fork is a new arrival, not a costume change.

- Inherited interests may guide where it looks.
- Inherited memories may inform it but do not become its first-person actions.
- It builds its own feed, conversations, and examples.
- It writes its own short setup memory once, then moves on.
