# Viral Opening Hooks

The first 15 seconds should create a concrete question the audience wants answered. The hook must be visual, not explanatory.

## 1. Formula Types

### identity_flip

The opening image contradicts who the protagonist appears to be.

> A respected surgeon steals a child's lunchbox from a hospital fridge.

### outcome_first

Show a shocking result first, then let the story explain how it happened.

> A bride in full makeup digs a grave behind the wedding venue.

### in_medias_res

Start inside an active conflict rather than before it.

> A man runs through a hotel corridor wearing only one shoe while guests applaud behind him.

### taboo_break

Begin with a violation the audience knows should not happen.

> A teacher sells exam answers to one student and gives the money to another.

### ticking_clock

Begin with visible time pressure.

> The elevator display counts down from 10 while everyone inside refuses to move.

### surreal_shock

Begin with an impossible or absurd image that still has emotional logic.

> Every family portrait in the house has the father's face scratched out except one: the dog.

### emotional_impact

Begin with a small action that implies enormous emotional stakes.

> A woman carefully irons a shirt with a bullet hole in it.

## 2. Hook Requirements

A usable hook should:

- Be filmable in one or a few images
- Raise a specific question
- Connect to the story's core dramatic action
- Avoid explaining the premise in dialogue
- Be paid off later, not just decorate the opening

## 3. Hook Writing Pattern

Write:

1. Formula name
2. First image
3. Immediate action
4. Question created
5. Later payoff

Example:

```markdown
## Opening Hook (First 15 Seconds)
- Formula: outcome_first
- First image: A woman parks at the cliff edge while her daughter sleeps in the back seat.
- Immediate action: She checks the mirror, smiles once, and shifts into drive.
- Question created: Why would she drive toward the cliff with her child in the car?
- Payoff: The insurance beneficiary and custody threat reveal the logic of the choice.
```

## 4. Common Failures

- The hook is only a mood shot with no question
- The hook is explained by voice-over
- The hook has no later payoff
- The hook belongs to a different genre than the rest of the story
- The hook is shocking but not tied to the protagonist's Want or Conflict

## 5. Revision Test

If the first 15 seconds were all the audience saw, would they ask a specific question and want the next 15 seconds? If not, rewrite the hook.
