# Audiovisual Language Arsenal (Detailed, Shared by Both Paths)

Ultra-short films are a legitimate space for audiovisual bravura. Under 3 minutes, audiences have high tolerance for formal experimentation. But follow two iron rules:

1. **Form serves content.** Decide the concept first, then choose audiovisual techniques. Do not start from "I want split screen" and then search for a concept to fit it.
2. **Focus on 1-2 core techniques and push them to the limit.** Cramming 5 audiovisual techniques into 3 minutes creates a showreel, not a short film.

Audiovisual technique descriptions must be concrete. Not "use a long take," but "the camera starts on the character's hand and slowly pulls back, revealing the whole room: every workstation is empty."

---

## 1. Camera Language

**Long Take / Oner**: no cuts from beginning to end; one continuous shot carries the whole film.

- Effect: realism / immersion / irreversible time.
- Fits: concepts that need a sense of presence, or concepts where the audience experiences time with the character.

**Locked-Off Time Passage**: the camera never moves; time accelerates through the frame.

- Effect: contrast between change and constancy; the only constant is the viewpoint.
- Fits: concepts with large time spans (100 years of a street corner / four seasons of a tree).

**Continuous Dolly**: the camera moves at a steady speed through space.

- Effect: spatial reveal; the audience keeps discovering new information.
- Fits: concepts where space itself carries information (mansion to slum / office to factory).

**First-Person POV**: the whole film is the subjective perspective of a character or object.

- Effect: extreme embodiment and restricted information.
- Fits: Perspective Shift How-to-Tell / What-If concepts that require being inside the experience.

**Macro / Microscope Shots**: film at scales invisible to the naked eye.

- Effect: the familiar becomes strange; a drop of water becomes an ocean.
- Fits: Scale Jump How-to-Tell.

---

## 2. Editing / Time

**Jump Cut Montage**: rapid time jumps within the same frame.

- Effect: time compression / absurdity of repetition.
- Fits: showing "day after day" concepts / quickly extrapolating consequences of a rule.

**Split Screen**: the frame is divided into 2-4 areas showing different content simultaneously.

- Effect: contrast / juxtaposition / parallel timelines.
- Fits: dual-line juxtaposition structures / inverted juxtaposition combination method.

**Cross Cutting**: quickly switch between two or more lines.

- Effect: accelerated contrast / accumulated tension / implied connection.
- Fits: two seemingly unrelated things that are eventually revealed to be connected.

**Reverse**: footage plays backward.

- Effect: the familiar becomes strange / result precedes cause.
- Fits: Time Surgery How-to-Tell / loop structures.

**Freeze Frame**: freeze the image at a key instant.

- Effect: forces attention to detail / creates suspense or punctuation.
- Fits: emphasizing the twist instant inside dense visual information.

---

## 3. Composition

**Symmetrical Composition**: the frame is strictly symmetrical.

- Effect: order / ritual / unease (over-perfect order implies oppression).
- Fits: institutionalized / rule-driven concepts.

**Foreground / Background Storytelling**: the foreground does one thing while the background does another; they annotate each other.

- Effect: juxtaposition without cutting; one frame reveals two layers of information.
- Fits: concepts that need juxtaposition without split screen.

**Color Constraint**: the whole film uses only a limited set of colors (even only black-and-white + one color).

- Effect: a visual "rule"; color itself becomes a narrative element.
- Fits: concepts directly linked to color (red = danger / gray = system).

---

## 4. Sound

**Image-Sound Dislocation**: the image shows A while the sound belongs to B.

- Effect: collision of two information layers.
- Fits: satire / contrast (peaceful everyday image with disaster statistics from a news broadcast).

**Amplified Ambient Sound**: everyday sounds become abnormally loud (keyboard / breath / swallowing).

- Effect: the familiar becomes unsettling.
- Fits: creating "horror inside the everyday."

**Silence**: complete absence of sound at a key moment.

- Effect: forces attention / creates a vacuum.
- Fits: sudden silence after dense information flow; the silence itself becomes the climax.

---

## 5. Interface / Media

**Screen Recording**: the whole film is a phone / computer screen recording.

- Effect: intensely contemporary; the audience feels like they are spying on someone's phone.
- Fits: Form Simulation How-to-Tell.

**Mock Document / Mockumentary**: pretend to be a real documentary / news report / instructional video.

- Effect: a "serious" form wraps an absurd core, making the absurdity stronger.
- Fits: What-If concepts that need a solemn shell as contrast.

**Data Visualization**: tell the story through charts / data / statistical visual language.

- Effect: huge tragedy behind calm, objective numbers; the colder the numbers, the stronger the shock.
- Fits: concepts that use "objectivity" to create contrast.

---

## Progression and Reversal of Audiovisual Techniques

Audiovisual techniques cannot stay unchanged from beginning to end. They can:

- **Upgrade**: a single split screen becomes a four-way split screen.
- **Mutate**: forward playback becomes reverse playback.
- **Break at the ending**: the whole film uses a locked-off camera, then the final shot suddenly moves.
- **Complete the twist themselves** (highest-level use): the whole film is color, then turns black-and-white after the twist; the whole film runs at normal speed, then the entire world flies past in the final second; the whole film is disguised as a documentary, then the final second reveals it is CGI.
