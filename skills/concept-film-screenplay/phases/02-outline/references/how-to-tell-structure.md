# How-to-Tell Path: Universal Three-Part Structure

Form is structure. The structure is determined by the narrative form itself. Universal rhythm:

```
1. Form Establishment (first 20-30 seconds)
   Let the audience understand "what method this film uses to tell the story."
   The form itself should feel fresh. The audience's first reaction should be, "Oh? Tell it this way?"

2. Form Deepening (middle 60-120 seconds)
   Develop the content inside the form's frame.
   The form itself progresses: it upgrades / mutates / exposes a new meaning.

3. Convergence of Form and Content (final 15-30 seconds)
   The audience realizes the form and content are one.
   "Telling it this way" is not packaging; it is part of the story itself.
   The highest-level How-to-Tell lets the form itself complete the twist.
```

## Key Judgments

- If the audience does not get the form within 15-30 seconds, the whole film fails. The form must teach itself.
- The second part cannot merely "keep displaying the form." The form must evolve, or the audience becomes numb by minute two.
- The third part's "convergence" is the lifeline of How-to-Tell. After watching, the user should feel "this content can only be told through this form," not "the form is pretty packaging."
