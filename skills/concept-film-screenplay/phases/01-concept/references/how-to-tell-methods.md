# How-to-Tell Path: 5 Creative Methods (Detailed)

The core of How-to-Tell is not "what to tell" because the content is already known. The core is "how to tell it." **Form is content**: if this form is removed, the story no longer works.

> **Path entry**: this file is used only in Phase 1 Step 4B (How-to-Tell path). If the path is What-If, read `what-if-combinations.md` instead.

---

## 1. Perspective Shift

Tell the story from an impossible perspective.

**Examples**:

- Tell a war from a bullet's point of view.
- Tell a day in the life of someone living alone from the point of view of a delivery bag.
- Tell the history of human civilization from Earth's perspective, as if Earth were a host infected by parasites.
- Tell the life of a meme as it is sent, received, screenshotted, and forwarded.

---

## 2. Form Simulation

Repackage the story through an existing form, such as a file format / interface / game / manual.

**Examples**:

- Tell a marriage in the format of a product manual (product name: marriage; shelf life: uncertain; side effects: see appendix).
- Tell a worker's day as a game walkthrough.
- Tell a person's gradual social isolation through app-interface interactions.
- Tell a convenience-store love story through security-camera footage.
- Tell a person's journey from diagnosis to acceptance through search history.

---

## 3. Time Surgery

Operate on time: speed up / slow down / reverse / loop / jump / juxtapose different times.

**Examples**:

- Compress a person's entire life into 2 minutes.
- Stretch a 1-second action into 3 minutes (what happens inside the 1 second of a bullet firing or a cup shattering).
- Show 100 years of the same street corner, cutting every 10 years while the camera position stays fixed.
- Play one person's morning forward and the same person's night backward; the two timelines meet at noon.

---

## 4. Scale Jump

Jump between the extremely large and the extremely small, using scale contrast to create impact.

**Examples**:

- Start with a cell dividing, then pull back step by step to a person / city / planet / galaxy, then cut back to the cell.
- A family argument -> cut to Earth from outer space -> cut back to the argument -> it suddenly becomes ridiculous.
- A grain of rice's journey: rice field -> dining table -> trash can -> landfill -> groundwater -> rice field.

---

## 5. Rule Constraint

Place a rule constraint on the narrative itself; the constraint creates the creativity.

**Examples**:

- The entire film uses only one fixed camera position and no cuts.
- No person appears in the whole film; only spaces and objects.
- The whole film has only sound and a black image (or the reverse).
- The whole film is one uninterrupted dolly shot.
- The whole film is made of phone screenshots / chat logs / notification panels.

---

## Selection Tips

- There is no formula for which method to choose. Prefer the option that **only works when told through this form**. If another form would produce roughly the same effect, this form is invalid packaging; do not choose it.
- The form must have room to progress. It cannot repeat one trick from beginning to end. Beginning / middle / ending must let the form upgrade / mutate / reveal a new meaning (see Phase 2 "progression and reversal" in audiovisual language).
- If unclear, brainstorm 3 candidates using different methods and let the user choose.
