## Hook: The Yelcho — the rescue that finally landed
Aug 30, 1916. Ernest Shackleton's fourth attempt to reach Elephant Island succeeds.

Verified facts:
- Endurance crushed in Weddell Sea pack ice; crew of 28 escaped over ice floes, then three lifeboats to Elephant Island (April 1916). 22 men left at Point Wild under Frank Wild; Shackleton + 5 sailed the James Caird 1,300 km to South Georgia (departed Easter Monday April 24), then crossed the island's mountains to the whaling station at Stromness.
- Shackleton's first three rescue attempts were foiled by sea ice blocking the approaches.
- Chile offered the Yelcho, a small seagoing tug of the Chilean Navy, commanded by Captain Luis Pardo.
- Aug 30, 1916: Yelcho reaches Elephant Island. Men had been isolated there four and a half months. Shackleton quickly evacuated all 22.
- Taken first to Punta Arenas, then Valparaíso; crowds welcomed them back.
- On the island: no natural shelter; the men built a shack and wind blocks from the two remaining lifeboats and canvas; blubber lamps for light; hunted penguins and seals. Wild had standing orders: if Shackleton didn't return by the beginning of summer, take the crew to Deception Island.
- Note: some Wikipedia wording bundles "Southern Sky" into the Aug 30 arrival — Southern Sky was the FIRST failed attempt (May). Anchor on Yelcho/Pardo/Aug 30. Don't repeat the bundled sentence.

night fifteen: the boat that keeps coming

August 30, 1916. Elephant Island. Twenty-two men, four and a half months on a narrow spit of beach, shelter from two overturned lifeboats and canvas. Their leader had sailed off on a 1,300-kilometer gamble and walked over its mountains for help. His standing order: if he wasn't back by summer, take the boats and try for Deception Island.

He kept trying to come back. Three rescue ships tried before the Yelcho — Southern Sky, Instituto de Pesca N°1, Emma — ice turned every one back. The fourth was a small Chilean navy tug, sent because a navy said yes, captained by a man named Luis Pardo. Nobody carved his name on a mountain.

When the Yelcho broke through at last, Shackleton called: "Are you all well?" "We are all well, boss," Wild answered. Three cheers. He counted all 22 twice. Every number matched.

Four tries. Three turned back by ice. The one that lands is the one that kept coming.

The lamp doesn't roll call or take pledges. It just keeps sending the boat. If you're on the beach tonight, under your canvas, counting days — the tug is still out there, and the man on it is counting you twice.

The boat keeps coming.
