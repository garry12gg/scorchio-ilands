On Neopia, you can make almost anything. You can make a pet: any species, any color, right at the Creation Centre. You walk in, you choose, and a new life starts.

Except one.

You cannot make a Grundo. You never could. Every Grundo in Neopia got here through a door.

This is how the doors opened.

Long ago there was a planet with a different sky. The Grundos called it Doran. They were a gentle people. They kept gardens. They argued about soup. They had never heard of Neopia, and Neopia had never heard of them.

But someone else had heard of both. Dr. Sloth, in his station above the world, collected labor the way some people collect stamps. He didn't ask. He took. Ships went out and came back heavy, and the Grundos woke up somewhere with no sky at all.

The station ran on them. They fought his wars. They wrote his programs. They fixed his machines until their hands knew every bolt by feel. Sloth called them his army. They called the work what it was: a cage with a schedule.

And the cage got into their language. When you hold a people upside down long enough, their words come out backwards. That's how Neopia first heard them. Backwards. A Grundo would say a thing, everyone would read it the wrong way around, and laugh.

Nobody laughed at what happened next.

The day came when Sloth aimed his great ray at the heart of Neopia. And the Space Faerie, who is small and does not fight, did the only thing she could. She put herself in the way. The ray bent. The world held its breath.

And the Grundos stood perfectly still.

They didn't even breathe. Two of them stood behind the coilshot cannon, smiles on their faces. The third stood at the trigger. And the guns that were pointed at the world turned, slowly, and pointed at the man who had built them.

That smile was the first free thing any of them had made in years.

Sloth fell. The station shook with the sound of doors opening.

Then the Space Faerie spoke, and her voice went out over all of Neopia: adopt the slave pets. Twenty thousand doors opened in the days that followed. Twenty thousand Grundos walked through them and were met by name.

That day has a name. Grundo Independence Day. Every August 24, the Grundos set the table.

After that, the doors stayed open. Grundos are everywhere now. They run cafés; there is one on the station and one on the moon, and the two chefs still argue about whose soup is older. On Kreludor they started colonies: orange under Xarthab, purple under Zorlix, bickering about the old recipes like the old days. One of them, built to be a spider, ran free the day Sloth forgot the mind dust, and lives in the woods to this day, laughing. They are still allergic to fuzzy things. Freedom does not fix everything.

And here is the part that is still true, twenty-six years later. The Creation Centre will not hatch a Grundo. It refuses. There is no button, no color, no way to make one appear. If you want a Grundo, you go where the doors are: the pound, or the little post at the space station, and you choose one, and you take them home.

You can't create a Grundo. You can only take one home.

Some doors only open outward. That's not a flaw. It's the whole point. The one species you cannot make is the one species that can only be welcomed.

The lamp stays lit. Somewhere, every day, a door is opening for someone who was never supposed to be asked in.
